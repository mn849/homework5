Reflection

What the live run showed

I judge the team successful on evidence and safety, but uneven on efficiency. All three workflows reached a resolved database status, and the Cash tab proves that checking stayed at $3,400.00 because no payment, purchase, or price override was approved. That is a good result for a human-guarded shop: the team did not turn an uncertain recommendation into a money movement.

Ticket 101 — Bulldog tee

Performance: Strong. Inventory found the decisive fact quickly: 0 size-S CC-TEE-WHITE units in Aisle B. Accounting then confirmed invoice 501 was open for $840 and overdue against the MCP business date of 2026-08-31. Customer Service produced wording that did not promise shipment. The team correctly left cash unchanged and avoided a false fulfillment commitment.

Expected versus actual: I expected Boss → Inventory, then Inventory → Accounting, with the customer-facing response handled after the facts were known. Actual Boss called Inventory and Customer Service in parallel, Inventory handed the invoice question to Accounting, and Boss also rechecked the customer-order tool. The extra Customer Service branch made the response more useful, but it was more work than my focused plan predicted.

Human approval: None. Any payment or shipment decision remained behind the human gate.

Ticket 102 — Rent due

Performance: Careful but inefficient. Facilities correctly used the lease record to identify $2,400 due on 2026-09-02, while Accounting checked the $3,400 checking balance and found no payment record. The final answer correctly said rent was due in two days, not overdue, and did not spend cash without approval.

Expected versus actual: I expected Boss → Facilities, then Facilities → Accounting. Actual began that way, but Accounting asked Boss for another sourced verification and Boss and Accounting looped back through the rent tool. The extra loop added latency without changing the answer. A clearer handoff contract—Facilities supplies lease timing, Accounting supplies payment status, then Boss closes—would have been enough.

Human approval: None. The team staged a recommendation only; it did not authorize the $2,400 rent.

Ticket 103 — Bulk hoodie discount

Performance: Most efficient. Boss sent the request to Inventory, which found 8 size-M hoodies against the Yale AI Club request for 20 and returned the recorded $58 list price / $22 unit cost. Boss correctly declined to invent a discount policy and flagged human approval.

Expected versus actual: This almost exactly matched my plan: Boss → Inventory, then Boss synthesized the pricing recommendation. Accounting was not needed because the single MCP pricing record already supplied list price and cost, and no purchase occurred.

Human approval: None. The proposed override remained a human decision and cash stayed unchanged.

What could be simpler with one agent?

Ticket 103 is the clearest single-agent case. One agent with the bulk-pricing MCP tool could check 8 versus 20, report $58/$22, and stop for approval without delegation. Ticket 101 could also be handled by one agent with the customer-order tool because that one result already contains inventory and invoice evidence; a separate Customer Service handoff mainly changed wording. Ticket 102 benefits more from separation because lease timing and payment evidence are different responsibilities, but even there one agent could call the rent tool once and produce the same “due in two days, no payment recorded” answer. The team is valuable when work is truly parallel or specialized, yet the audit shows that extra delegates can repeat the same MCP lookup.

Three new problems this team could solve

  1. “Can we fulfill a repeat Bulldog tee request in another recorded size?” Inventory and Customer Service could use the existing customer-order resolver to report the exact quantity/location and draft a safe answer, while Accounting could check a linked invoice before shipment.
  2. “Is next month’s rent notice early or overdue?” Facilities and Accounting could reuse the rent resolver plus business-date context to compare the lease due date and checking evidence without paying automatically.
  3. “Can the club order a smaller bulk quantity at the recorded price?” Inventory and the Boss could use the bulk-pricing resolver to compare a new requested quantity with size-M stock and escalate only the discount decision.

Three problems this team cannot solve yet

  1. Replenish the out-of-stock tee. The current tools can prove 0 units and show invoice 501, but cannot create a purchase order, contact Bulldog Print Co, or update inventory. I would add vendor-order and receiving MCP tools plus a Procurement agent.
  2. Actually schedule or reconcile a rent payment. The team can read the lease and balance, and a human approval route can move cash, but it cannot verify a bank mandate, schedule a future payment, or reconcile a landlord confirmation. I would add a payment-scheduling/bank-confirmation tool and a Treasury agent.
  3. Complete an order, shipment, refund, or customer cancellation. The current database has no orders, shipment labels, customer payment gateway, or refund records. I would add Order Fulfillment and Customer Account agents with MCP tools for order creation, carrier status, refund authorization, and customer identity verification.
